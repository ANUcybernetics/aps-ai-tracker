# Australian Electoral Commission Artificial Intelligence (AI) Transparency Statement

## Background

The Australian Electoral Commission (AEC) notes the Policy for the Responsible Use of AI in Government (the Policy) requires that relevant agencies, including the AEC, release a transparency statement providing information about their use of AI.

The AEC's AI Accountable Official is the Deputy Electoral Commissioner. The AEC has also appointed the Chief Information Officer to the role of the Chief AI Officer in recognition of the fundamental shift that generative AI is bringing to government operations and as required in the AI Plan for the Australian Public Service.

## The AEC's use of AI

AI presents opportunities as well as risks for government. For the AEC, consideration of these opportunities must reflect the sensitivity of electoral administration, the importance of reliable and explainable processes, and the need to maintain public confidence. Upholding the integrity, transparency, and assurance of electoral processes, and the trust of stakeholders, is paramount when considering the use of AI in the AEC.

The AEC is taking a cautious, risk-based and context-sensitive approach to AI. We will only consider its use where it is appropriate, supports our people and services, and maintains trust in the electoral system. Staff using AEC systems are required to complete mandatory AI training to support awareness of the safe and responsible use of AI. We have an internal policy on the use of AI, which staff are required to follow when using AEC approved AI tools. This policy requires and assists staff to:

- treat AI-generated outputs as advisory, apply critical judgement before relying on them, and remain accountable for decisions and outcomes
- understand safe and responsible use of AI in accordance with Australia's AI ethics framework
- not use AI to independently provide authoritative advice or make decisions in public facing interactions
- comply with the AEC's AI in recruitment guidelines, which set expectations for the appropriate, transparent and ethical use of AI in recruitment activities. The guidelines are informed by the Australian Public Service Commission's recruitment principles for agencies and candidates.

The AEC also has an AI assurance assessment process in place to assess potential AI tools and use based on the Pilot Australian Government AI assurance framework. Approval of an AI tool does not mean all capabilities or uses of that tool are approved. AI features and uses are assessed according to their business need, purpose, context and risk.

The AEC's use of AI includes:

- GitHub Copilot – GitHub Copilot is a generative AI coding assistant that supports technical staff with software development tasks, including writing, understanding and reviewing code. Developers remain responsible for reviewing, validating and approving AI-generated outputs before they are incorporated into AEC systems or software.
- Microsoft 365 Copilot – The AEC conducted a limited trial of Microsoft 365 Copilot (licensed version) to evaluate its potential to support productivity and corporate work. Following the trial, Microsoft 365 Copilot remains available to participating staff for approved workplace productivity uses, subject to the AEC AI Use Policy and human review of AI-generated outputs.
- Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat – The AEC has enabled Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat for staff to support workplace productivity and collaboration. Staff remain responsible for reviewing and validating AI-generated outputs and for the work they produce. Copilot Chat operates within Microsoft 365's enterprise data protection controls, and prompts and responses are not used to train the underlying foundation models.
- GovAI Chat Trial – The AEC is participating in the whole-of-government GovAI Chat trial, operated by the Department of Finance, through evaluation involving a limited and targeted cohort of staff. The trial offers a secure environment specifically designed for Australian Public Service (APS) agencies to test the new AI capability , while maintaining government data sovereignty requirements.
- Other – Some AEC staff have access to assistive and adaptive technologies such as speech-to-text software along with a limited number of staff in the AEC's design area who use AI-assisted design features to support the development of educational materials. These features assist the design process and do not replace human design, review and approval of final materials.

The AEC uses AI in the following domain and usage pattern:

- Domain: Corporate and enabling. The AEC's current use relates to supporting corporate functions and improving operational efficiency.
- Usage pattern: Workplace productivity. The AEC's current use is for workplace productivity purposes. This includes summarising and analysing data and information, automating or reducing time spent on administrative tasks and supporting software application development.

More information about usage patterns and domains is available at Classification system for AI use | digital.gov.au

## Identifying and protecting against risks to electoral integrity

The AEC acknowledges the transformative potential of AI for society and government, including operational efficiencies and enhanced data analysis. The 2024 Australian Government trial of Microsoft 365 Copilot showed productivity improvements but also highlighted barriers, concerns, and risks associated with AI in government.

Public trust in the AEC is crucial for maintaining confidence in electoral processes and results, which is essential for defending Australia's democracy. In a rapidly evolving digital landscape, meeting community and stakeholder expectations is increasingly complex.

To maintain public confidence, the AEC emphasises the importance of transparency in how AI is used in its operations, security, and information systems as detailed above in "The AEC's use of AI".

The AEC will not use AI to exercise statutory powers or make autonomous electoral, regulatory or compliance decisions reserved for authorised human decision makers. Key election operations, including voting and vote counting, are undertaken in accordance with electoral law without the use of AI. The AEC will not use AI to make autonomous decisions about voting eligibility, vote counting or election outcomes.

## Third-party suppliers

The AEC has processes in place to manage the potential use of AI by third-party suppliers.

The AEC requires all vendors to comply with relevant contractual obligations throughout the lifecycle of a contract, including requirements relating to information security, confidentiality, privacy, and the protection of personal information.

Where applicable, the AEC additionally requires all relevant provider personnel are Australian citizens and may require that personnel possess Australian Government security clearances at a level the AEC deems appropriate per-role.

For applicable agreements (i.e. managed service arrangements), the AEC contractually requires that AEC data remains onshore in Australia.

In cases where the provider is delivering an IT capability that processes AEC data, such solutions are subject to the AEC's review and approval through its governance processes. Any potential utilisation of AI as part of such solutions would be identified as part of this process, including with respect to potential data sovereignty issues.

## Governance and legislation

### Governance Structure

Senior Executive Committee:

- overarching monitoring of performance, accountability, risk, and agency direction
- chaired by the Australian Electoral Commissioner
- approves AI tools and use at the AEC in conjunction with the Corporate Enabling Committee.

Corporate Enabling Committee:

- oversees the AEC's Use of AI Policy
- links operational governance and executive decision-making, providing an escalation pathway to the Senior Executive Committee.

Advisory Audit Committee:

- established under the Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013 (PGPA Act).

### Compliance with Legislation and Regulation

- Privacy Act 1988: Regulates the collection, storage, and use of personal information by AI
- Archives Act 1983: Governs records created by AI
- Freedom of Information Act 1982: Provides public access to documents created by AI.

### Oversight and Audits

- Compliance overseen by the Senior Executive Committee.
- Internal audits are conducted to detect non-compliance.
- The Regulatory Action Plan guides the AEC's approach to regulatory compliance, including application of the new AI Policy.

## Updates to this statement

Consistent with the Policy for the Responsible Use of AI in Government, this statement will be updated at least once a year, or when the AEC makes a substantial change to the agency's approach to AI, or when any new factor materially impacts the existing statement's accuracy. This statement was updated on 25 October 2026 and is authorised by AEC's AI Accountable Officer.
