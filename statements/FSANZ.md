Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ) recognises the potential for Artificial Intelligence (AI) to support innovation. This statement is part of our compliance with the [Policy for the responsible use of AI in government](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy), as adopted from 1 September 2024.

Please refer to the [FSANZ Corporate Plan 2026-27](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/publications/corporate-plan-2026-27) for our key activities that AI may impact over time.

## Our approach

FSANZ uses and explores artificial intelligence to support workplace productivity, improve access to information and help staff prepare analysis and draft material. People remain accountable for FSANZ work and decisions. Artificial intelligence supports, but does not replace, professional judgement, scientific assessment, regulatory responsibility, delegated authority or executive accountability.

FSANZ will only adopt artificial intelligence where there is a clear business purpose, proportionate governance, appropriate human oversight and controls to protect information, privacy, security, records, fairness and public trust.

### Accountable official

The Chief Information Officer is FSANZ’s accountable official for artificial intelligence governance. The accountable official oversees FSANZ’s approach to artificial intelligence adoption and use, the internal use case register, implementation of governance controls, monitoring and review of this statement.

### Why FSANZ has adopted this approach

FSANZ is a corporate Commonwealth entity. The Australian Government Policy for the responsible use of AI in government is mandatory for non-corporate Commonwealth entities and encourages corporate Commonwealth entities to apply it. FSANZ has voluntarily adopted the relevant requirements as a matter of good governance and public sector best practice.

FSANZ’s approach is intended to support safe and responsible innovation, strengthen public trust, improve productivity and ensure that artificial intelligence is used in a way that is lawful, ethical, secure, transparent and accountable.

How we currently use artificial intelligence

- Workplace productivity, including drafting, summarising, document review and information organisation.
- Research and analysis support, with human verification of sources, assumptions and conclusions.
- Administrative and enabling functions.
- Internal knowledge management and access to information, where approved tools and information handling controls are in place.

FSANZ does not treat artificial intelligence as a substitute for professional judgement. It is not used as the sole basis for regulatory, scientific, employment, compliance, procurement, financial or other consequential decisions.

### Public interaction and impact

At the date of publication, FSANZ has not approved any use where the public directly interacts with an artificial intelligence system without a human intermediary, or where a person is significantly affected by artificial intelligence without human review or intervention.

If this changes, FSANZ will update this statement and ensure that direct public interaction with an artificial intelligence system is clearly identified. FSANZ will assess the proposed use, identify potential impacts, apply proportionate safeguards, assign an owner, confirm the required monitoring and approval arrangements, and retain records to support accountability.

### Governance and safeguards

- A senior accountable official oversees implementation and review.
- Use cases are recorded in an internal register.
- New or changed use cases are assessed according to their purpose, impact and risk.
- Higher-risk uses require a detailed assessment, named owner, approval and monitoring.
- Only approved tools may be used for official business.
- Staff must verify outputs before relying on them and must protect personal, sensitive, classified, confidential and legally protected information.
- Artificial intelligence must not be used as the sole basis for consequential decisions.
- Material incidents and safety concerns are reported and managed through established processes.
- Records are retained where needed to explain significant decisions, actions, approvals, use case assessments and incident responses.
- Procurement of artificial intelligence products and services must consider risk, privacy, security, records, transparency, assurance, supplier management and value for money.

### Monitoring and effectiveness

FSANZ monitors the effectiveness and safe use of artificial intelligence by maintaining an internal register of assessed use cases; requiring use case owners to review controls and risks when there is a material change in purpose, data, system behaviour, supplier, automation level or impact; reviewing higher-risk use cases through appropriate governance channels; monitoring reported incidents, safety concerns and issues; reviewing this statement annually, or sooner if there is a significant change to FSANZ’s approach or use of artificial intelligence; and reviewing relevant policies, procedures and training when government guidance, risks or approved tools materially change.

### Compliance with laws, policies and standards

FSANZ uses artificial intelligence in a way that is intended to be consistent with applicable legal, regulatory and public sector obligations. Depending on the use case, this may include obligations relating to privacy, secrecy, security, records management, accessibility, copyright, procurement, employment, public administration, the Australian Public Service Code of Conduct and FSANZ’s internal governance requirements.

FSANZ’s responsible use of artificial intelligence framework is intended to complement existing controls and does not replace normal legal, policy, procurement, information management, security, privacy, scientific, regulatory or financial governance requirements.

### Protecting the public against negative impacts

FSANZ manages the risk of negative impacts from artificial intelligence by requiring human accountability for official work and decisions; validation of outputs before they are relied on; proportionate assessment of proposed artificial intelligence use cases; controls for privacy, security, records, fairness, bias, accuracy and accessibility; clear disclosure where a person interacts directly with an artificial intelligence system; reporting and escalation of incidents, harmful outputs and safety concerns; and review of use cases where there are material changes in purpose, data, system operation, supplier or impact.

### Public reporting and enquiries

Questions or concerns about FSANZ’s use of artificial intelligence may be directed to FSANZ through the agency’s published contact channels. Where a concern relates to privacy, security, records, regulatory process, scientific assessment, procurement, employment or another specific matter, FSANZ will manage the concern through the relevant existing process.

### Publication, notification and review

FSANZ will publish this statement on its website once approved. FSANZ will review and update the statement at least annually, or earlier if there is a significant change to its approach to artificial intelligence or if new factors materially affect the accuracy of the statement.

Where FSANZ publishes or materially updates this statement as part of its voluntary adoption of the Australian Government policy settings, FSANZ will consider whether notification to the Digital Transformation Agency is appropriate.
